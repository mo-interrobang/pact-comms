04

The same consultant, the same pattern, in Saline Township

A different Michigan township's own court record shows Auerbach legitimizing a data-center application under a zoning provision that does not exist — one his own ordinance required him to reject outright — and shows him on a litigation-strategy call with the township's outside counsel the day its board overrode its own denial with a consent judgment. Saline terminated Carlisle/Wortman afterward.

Exhibit ESaline Township Zoning Ordinance, Art. 12 §12.04(A)

Citation Exhibit C to Haushalter Reply Brief, Bates 085  Effective Feb. 1, 2008

“Conditional rezoning, as authorized by Section 405 of the Michigan Zoning Enabling Act, shall be prohibited in Saline Township. Any application for a rezoning amendment to the Official Zoning Map that includes proposed conditions or voluntary use or development limitations shall be returned to the applicant without Township review or consideration.”
Exhibit F"Conditional Rezoning Review for Saline Township" — Carlisle/Wortman, signed Mike Auerbach, AICP

Citation Exhibit D to Haushalter Reply Brief, Bates 094  Date August 4, 2025

“Specific conditional rezoning standards are established in Section 12.04(A)(2) of the Zoning Ordinance. Those standards, and our comments on the proposal according to those standards are as follows...”

No such subsection exists. §12.04(A) runs directly from Prohibited (A) to Initiation (B); the standards Auerbach quoted and applied trace to a Conditional Rezoning Ordinance Amendment drafted in 2013, recommended by the Planning Commission that December, and never adopted by the Township Board — confirmed in writing by the Township Clerk's own FOIA response (Exhibit I, Bates 120–121: an adopting amendment "does not exist under the name provided... or by another name reasonably known to the Township").

Exhibit GAffidavit of LuAnne Kozma, sworn Jan. 30, 2026

Citation Exhibit 1 to Haushalter Reply Brief, ¶¶26–27  Filed Feb. 12, 2026, Washtenaw County Circuit Court, Case No. 25-001577-CZ

“[Auerbach] did not use the actual Saline Township Zoning Ordinance §12.04(A), which required him to return a conditional rezoning application to the applicant without Township review or consideration, and instead used a 12-year old draft that was never approved by Saline Township, and never became law.”

05

What's actually missing from the ordinance he drafted for Allen Park

Scoped precisely to Auerbach's own July 15, 2026 draft — still the latest version presented to the city as of the September 3 packet — two things are true regardless of how the ZBA appeal is ultimately resolved.

Vested-rights languageAbsent — not flagged as pending in his own transmittal memo
Small-Scale minimum lot2 acres
Small-Scale setback100 ft, all property lines
Lot coverage cap60%

No provision anywhere in the draft addresses what happens to a pending application — like Solstice's — once the moratorium lifts. And once the ordinance's own 60% lot-coverage cap and required parking, loading, and screening are factored against a 2-acre minimum lot with a 100 ft setback from every property line, it's fair to ask whether the Small-Scale pathway is a real compliance option or one narrow enough to function as a second, unstated prohibition.

The ask

Recusal from the dual role — managing the appeal's procedural status while drafting the ordinance that governs it — and an independent second opinion on the ordinance draft from a planner with no relationship to Carlisle/Wortman or an established relationship with Solstice.
